Input devices

What is an input device?

Hardware that captures data from outside and sends it into the computer. You don’t need to know how devices work – you need to choose one and justify it.

Manual

A person enters the data

  • Keyboard, keypad
  • Mouse, touchpad
  • Touchscreen
  • Microphone
  • Games controller

Automatic capture

Reads the data itself – faster, fewer errors

  • Barcode, QR scanner
  • RFID, NFC reader
  • Camera, ANPR
  • OMR, OCR scanner
  • Fingerprint reader

Sensors

Measure the world

  • Temperature, light
  • Pressure, moisture
  • Infra-red (motion)
  • Gas, pH, acoustic
  • Accelerometer, GPS

How to choose: N-U-J

  • Who? The user: age, training, disability, how many.
  • Where? Dirty, wet, noisy, outdoors, must stay clean.
  • What data? Text, sound, image, an ID code, a measurement.
  • How much? Volume, speed, accuracy, cost.

Worked example: a hospital must give each patient the right medicine.
Name: a handheld barcode scanner.
Use: scan the wristband and the medicine.
Justify: faster than typing and avoids errors, so the wrong medicine is less likely; it can be wiped clean.

Barcode, QR, RFID or NFC?

HoldsRangeGood for
BarcodeA short numberLine of sight, closeShop products
QR codeText or a web linkLine of sight, phone cameraMenus, tickets, posters
RFID tagAn ID, can be updatedUp to metres, no line of sight; many at onceStock, pets, library books
NFCAn ID or payment dataA few centimetresContactless payment

Sensors

  • Sensors take readings automatically, again and again – never tired, can work in danger.
  • Monitoring records or warns (heart rate on a ward). Control acts on the reading (air conditioning).
  • Accessibility inputs: eye tracker, sip and puff switch, foot mouse, microphone with speech recognition, braille keyboard, large key keyboard.

Watch out for:

  • Software instead of hardware: “a scanning app” gets no mark.
  • Brand names: “an iPad” – say “a touchscreen tablet”.
  • Two devices in one answer – only the first is marked.
  • Describing how a device works when asked why it suits the scenario.

Quick quiz – marked for you

1. A school must mark 300 multiple-choice answer sheets. AO2

Optical mark recognition reads marks in set places on a form: fast and accurate for multiple-choice sheets.

2. A surgeon needs to check scans mid-operation. AO2

Speech recognition keeps the surgeon’s hands sterile. The theatre is quiet, so speech is recognised well.

3. A library wants to check out a pile of books in one go. AO2

RFID needs no line of sight and reads many tags at once, so check-out is quick.

4. A user with very limited mobility in their hands and arms needs to send a signal. AO2

The user blows into a tube to send the signal – no hands needed. A mouse or keyboard needs hands.

5. What does an ADC do? AO1

Sensors usually give an analogue signal; an analogue-to-digital converter makes it digital for the processor.

6. Is “a barcode scanning app” credited as an input device? AO1

OCR mark schemes: the first mark must be hardware, not software. Say “a barcode scanner”.

Exam-style questions – mark them yourself

Write your answer, then open Mark your answer. Tick the mark points you hit, read the model answer and use the tips to improve. See the 1.1 exam tips for more help.

AO1[1 mark]

Explain what is meant by an input device.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: An input device is hardware that captures data from outside the computer and sends it into the computer.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Say hardware, and that it sends data into the computer.
AO2[4 marks]

A supermarket is installing self-service tills. Identify two input devices the tills need and explain why each is needed.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: A barcode scanner is needed to read each product’s code quickly, without the customer typing it, which avoids errors. Scales are needed to weigh loose fruit and vegetables and to check that the item put in the bagging area matches what was scanned.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Device (1) + reason (1), twice. One device per answer line.
  • Make each reason fit a self-service till, not just any shop.
AO3[3 marks]

A farmer wants to water crops only when the soil is dry. Recommend a suitable input device and justify your choice.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: A moisture sensor placed in the soil. It takes readings automatically, day and night, without the farmer checking. The watering system is only switched on when the reading falls below a set level, so water isn’t wasted.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Name the sensor and what it measures in this scenario.
  • Justify: why a sensor is better than a person here.

How to revise this

  • Comparison tables – barcode vs QR vs RFID vs NFC
  • Flashcards – device on the front, a scenario it suits on the back
  • Blurting – list every input device you can, by family

These are the best fit for this part of the topic, but you can use any revision technique that works for you.
