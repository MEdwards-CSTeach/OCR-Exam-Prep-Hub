1.4.3 Boolean algebra • Exam tips

1.4.3 exam tips

What examiners look for in Boolean algebra questions, with weak and good answers.

Why this topic matters

Paper 1 tests Boolean algebra almost every year, and it is mostly method marks. Learn the method and show every step.

What the examiner looks for

Rewarded

  • Naming the law at each step (Oct 2021).
  • 1 mark per correct K-map group (June 2025).
  • Brackets where needed (Oct 2021).
  • 1 mark per truth table column (Oct 2021).
  • Any accepted notation: AND, . and ∧ all score.

Loses marks

  • K-map columns 00, 01, 10, 11 – the wrong order.
  • Groups of 3 or 6, or diagonal groups.
  • Missing the wrap-around group.
  • Extra terms: “any additional symbols, max 2 marks”.
  • “When the clock changes” – say rising edge.

Question types and how to approach them

TypeHow to approach it
Write an expression from a descriptionOne letter per condition; AND for “while” / “and”, OR for “or”; brackets round each condition group.
Truth table2n rows, counted in binary. One column per gate, brackets first.
Simplify, naming lawsOne step per line, the law beside it. Stop when nothing else applies. Check with a truth table.
Karnaugh mapFill the map in Gray code; ring the groups; one term per group; join with ∨.
Describe a circuitName the inputs, name the outputs, say when and how the outputs change. For adders: half, full, chain, carry.

Count the marks: 4 marks means 4 separate points or steps.

Weak and good answers

Question: The circuit is a half adder. Describe how it can be adapted to add together two 4-bit binary numbers. [4] (Oct 2021)

Weak answer 1 mark

Use four of them joined together so it can add 4 bits.

Good answer 4 marks

S gives the sum and C the carry. Two half adders joined with an OR gate make a full adder. Four full adders add two 4-bit numbers, with the carry out of each joined to the carry in of the next.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Four half adders can’t pass on a carry – you need full adders.
  • Four steps, four marks: half adder, full adder, chain of four, carry link.

Question: Describe the inputs and outputs used by a D-type flip-flop, explaining how the inputs control the outputs. [4] (Nov 2020)

Weak answer 1 mark

It has an input and an output and it stores data when the clock changes.

Good answer 4 marks

It has a data input D and a clock input. The outputs are Q and ¬Q. On the rising edge of the clock, Q changes to the value of D. ¬Q is always the opposite of Q.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Name the inputs and outputs – “an input” earns nothing.
  • Replace “when the clock changes” with “on the rising edge”.

Question: Simplify ¬(¬A ∨ B) ∨ (A ∧ B), naming the law at each step. [3]

Weak answer 0–1 mark

= A

Good answer 3 marks

(¬¬A ∧ ¬B) ∨ (A ∧ B) [De Morgan’s] → (A ∧ ¬B) ∨ (A ∧ B) [double negation] → A ∧ (¬B ∨ B) [distribution] → A [complement, identity].

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • The marks are for the steps and the named laws, not just the answer.
  • One law per line keeps the method clear.

Question: Use a Karnaugh map to simplify Q, where Q is 1 for ABC = 010, 011, 110 and 111. [3]

Weak answer 1 mark

Columns written 00, 01, 10, 11, and two groups of 2: (¬A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ B).

Good answer 3 marks

Columns in Gray code 00, 01, 11, 10. The four 1s form one group of 4 in which only B stays the same: Q = B.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Use Gray code so neighbouring cells really are neighbours.
  • Make groups as big as possible – a group of 4 drops two variables.

In the exam room

  • Write the five laws and the toolkit rules at the top of the page before you start.
  • Draw K-map axes in Gray code first, then fill them in.
  • Check any simplification with a quick truth table: both sides must match on every row.
  • Practise with worked examples, and learn the laws with flashcards.
