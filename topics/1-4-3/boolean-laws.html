The five laws on the specification

LawAND formOR form
CommutationA ∧ B ≡ B ∧ AA ∨ B ≡ B ∨ A
Association(A ∧ B) ∧ C ≡ A ∧ (B ∧ C)(A ∨ B) ∨ C ≡ A ∨ (B ∨ C)
DistributionA ∧ (B ∨ C) ≡ (A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ C)A ∨ (B ∧ C) ≡ (A ∨ B) ∧ (A ∨ C)
Double negation¬¬A ≡ A
De Morgan’s¬(A ∧ B) ≡ ¬A ∨ ¬B¬(A ∨ B) ≡ ¬A ∧ ¬B

In the exam, name the law at each step. Naming it earns the marks.

De Morgan’s laws

Break the line, change the sign. Change ∧ to ∨ (or ∨ to ∧) and NOT each part. Never do only one of these.

In code: not (age < 13 or age > 19) is the same as age >= 13 and age <= 19.

Your simplifying toolkit

RuleANDORWhy
IdentityA ∧ 1 ≡ AA ∨ 0 ≡ A1 and 0 change nothing here
NullA ∧ 0 ≡ 0A ∨ 1 ≡ 1One input decides it
IdempotentA ∧ A ≡ AA ∨ A ≡ ASaying it twice adds nothing
ComplementA ∧ ¬A ≡ 0A ∨ ¬A ≡ 1Can never / always be true

Complement is the move that makes terms vanish: look for X and ¬X together. (Beyond the spec: absorption, A ∨ (A ∧ B) ≡ A.)

Layout: one step per line, the law beside it. Stop when nothing else applies, then check with a quick truth table.

Watch out for:

  • Not naming the law – no law, no method mark, even with the right answer.
  • De Morgan’s half done: changing the operator but not negating each part (or the reverse).
  • Doing several laws in one line without saying so.
  • Leaving extra symbols in the answer: “any additional symbols, max 2 marks”.

Quick quiz – marked for you

1. Which law is used? ¬(P ∨ Q) ≡ ¬P ∧ ¬Q AO1

Break the line, change the sign: De Morgan’s.

2. Which law is used? X ∧ Y ≡ Y ∧ X AO1

Commutation: the order doesn’t matter.

3. Which law is used? A ∧ (B ∨ C) ≡ (A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ C) AO1

Distribution: A is multiplied out across the bracket.

4. Simplify ¬(¬A ∨ ¬B). AO2

De Morgan’s gives ¬¬A ∧ ¬¬B; double negation gives A ∧ B.

5. Simplify (A ∧ B) ∨ (A ∧ ¬B). Give a single letter. AO2

Distribution: A ∧ (B ∨ ¬B). Complement: A ∧ 1. Identity: A.

6. Simplify A ∨ (¬A ∧ B). AO3

Distribution (OR form): (A ∨ ¬A) ∧ (A ∨ B). Complement: 1 ∧ (A ∨ B). Identity: A ∨ B.

7. Which is equivalent to ¬(A ∧ ¬B)? AO2

De Morgan’s: ¬A ∨ ¬¬B. Double negation: ¬A ∨ B.

Exam-style questions – mark them yourself

Write your answer, then open Mark your answer. Tick the mark points you hit, read the model answer and use the tips to improve. See the 1.4.3 exam tips for more help.

AO2[3 marks]

Simplify ¬(¬A ∨ B) ∨ (A ∧ B), naming the law at each step.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: (¬¬A ∧ ¬B) ∨ (A ∧ B) [De Morgan’s] → (A ∧ ¬B) ∨ (A ∧ B) [double negation] → A ∧ (¬B ∨ B) [distribution] → A ∧ 1 [complement] → A [identity].

How to improve a weak answer:

  • One step per line with the law beside it.
  • No law named means no method mark, even if the answer is right.
AO2[2 marks]

Simplify Q ≡ ¬(¬A ∧ ¬B) so that it contains no negation. (October 2021)

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: ¬¬A ∨ ¬¬B (De Morgan’s), then A ∨ B (double negation).

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Check the final answer has no ¬ left, as the question asks.
AO3[4 marks]

Simplify ¬(A ∧ ¬B) ∧ A, naming each law. State which single gate the result represents.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: (¬A ∨ ¬¬B) ∧ A [De Morgan’s] → (¬A ∨ B) ∧ A [double negation] → A ∧ (¬A ∨ B) [commutation] → (A ∧ ¬A) ∨ (A ∧ B) [distribution] → 0 ∨ (A ∧ B) [complement] → A ∧ B [identity]. This is an AND gate.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Apply one law at a time and rewrite the whole line.
  • Look for X ∧ ¬X – complement makes the term vanish.

How to revise this

  • Flashcards – each law in both forms
  • Worked examples – simplify one step at a time, naming the law
  • Mnemonics – De Morgan’s: “break the line, change the sign”

These are the best fit for this part of the topic, but you can use any revision technique that works for you.
