Adders and D-type flip-flops

The logic of half adders, full adders and D-type flip-flops: circuits that add and remember.

The half adder

  • Adds two bits. 2 inputs (A, B), 2 outputs (S, C).
  • Sum S ≡ A ⊻ B (XOR). Carry C ≡ A ∧ B (AND).
  • “Half” because it has no carry in, so it can only add the first column.

The full adder

  • Adds two bits and a carry in. 3 inputs (A, B, Cin), 2 outputs (S, Cout).
  • Half adder 1 adds A and B. Half adder 2 adds the carry in. The OR gate gives a carry out if either half adder carried.
  • Ripple carry adder: chain full adders, joining each carry out to the next carry in. Four full adders add two 4-bit numbers. 1011 + 0110 = 1 0001: the final carry out of 1 means overflow.

The D-type flip-flop

  • Stores 1 bit. It delays a signal until the clock says go.
  • Inputs: D (data) and the clock. Outputs: Q, and ¬Q, which is always its opposite.
  • On the rising edge of the clock (0 → 1), Q becomes D.
  • At all other times Q holds its value, even if D changes.
  • Used in registers, memory and counters. 8 flip-flops sharing a clock store a byte.

Watch out for:

  • “Four half adders” to add 4 bits – half adders can’t pass on a carry.
  • Mixing up the gates: sum is XOR, carry is AND.
  • Saying Q changes “when the clock changes” – say the rising edge.
  • Not naming the inputs and outputs of the flip-flop.

Quick quiz – marked for you

1. Which gate gives the sum output of a half adder? AO1

1 + 0 and 0 + 1 give sum 1; 1 + 1 gives sum 0 (carry 1). That is XOR.

2. Which gate gives the carry output of a half adder? AO1

There is only a carry when both bits are 1: AND.

3. A full adder has A = 1, B = 1, Cin = 1. What is the sum S? AO2

1 + 1 + 1 = 11 in binary: sum 1, carry out 1.

4. Four full adders add 1011 + 0110. What is the final carry out? AO2

1011 + 0110 = 1 0001. The carry out of 1 means the answer needs 5 bits: overflow.

5. When does the Q output of a D-type flip-flop change? AO1

Q only changes on the rising edge of the clock. Between edges it holds its value.

6. Why can’t a half adder add the second column of two binary numbers? AO3

The second column must add the carry from the first. A full adder has the extra carry in input.

Exam-style questions – mark them yourself

Write your answer, then open Mark your answer. Tick the mark points you hit, read the model answer and use the tips to improve. See the 1.4.3 exam tips for more help.

AO2[4 marks]

A circuit is a half adder. Describe how it can be adapted to add together two 4-bit binary numbers. (October 2021)

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: S gives the sum and C the carry. Two half adders joined with an OR gate make a full adder, which also adds a carry in. Four full adders can add two 4-bit numbers, with the carry out of each one joined to the carry in of the next.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Four steps for four marks: half adder → full adder → chain of four → carry link.
  • Four half adders on their own can’t pass on a carry.
AO1[4 marks]

Describe the inputs and outputs used by a D-type flip-flop, explaining how the inputs control the outputs. (November 2020)

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: It has a data input D and a clock input. The outputs are Q and ¬Q. When the clock goes from low to high – the rising edge – Q changes to the value of D, and holds it until the next rising edge. ¬Q is always the opposite of Q.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Name the inputs, name the outputs, then explain when they change.
  • “When the clock changes” is too vague – say the rising edge.
AO2[2 marks]

Explain why a half adder cannot be used to add the second column of two binary numbers.

Mark your answer

Tick each mark point your answer includes.

Model answer: A half adder only has two inputs, A and B, and no carry in, so the carry from the first column would be lost.

How to improve a weak answer:

  • Say what is missing and what goes wrong because of it.

How to revise this

  • Trace tables – trace P, R, T, S and Cout through the full adder
  • Flowcharts and process diagrams – draw the half adder → full adder → ripple carry chain
  • Teach it – explain the timing diagram out loud

These are the best fit for this part of the topic, but you can use any revision technique that works for you.
