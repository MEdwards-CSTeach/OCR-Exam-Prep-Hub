1.4.1 Data types • Exam tips

1.4.1 exam tips

What examiners look for in data representation questions, with weak and good answers.

Why this topic matters

1.4.1 questions are mostly calculations with right or wrong answers – reliable marks if you practise. There is no calculator, so you must be quick by hand.

What the examiner looks for

Rewarded

  • Working shown – method marks are often available.
  • The exact number of bits asked for, with leading 0s (or 1s).
  • The right method: two’s complement vs sign and magnitude; normalised floating point.
  • For masks: both the operator and the mask.
  • For explanations: a reason with an example (3 + −3 = −6 in sign and magnitude).

Loses marks

  • A bare answer with a slip and no working.
  • Dropping leading zeros: 101 instead of 0000 0101.
  • Moving the point the wrong way, or adjusting the exponent the wrong way.
  • “Hex is used because computers understand it.”
  • Confusing the character '5' with the integer 5, or KB with KiB.

Weak and good answers

Question: Convert −45 into an 8-bit two’s complement binary number. Show your working. [2]

Weak answer 0 marks

10101101

Good answer 2 marks

45 = 0010 1101. Flip: 1101 0010. Add 1: 1101 0011. Check: −128 + 64 + 16 + 2 + 1 = −45.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • The weak answer is sign and magnitude – read which method is asked for.
  • Show each step so a slip still earns a method mark.
  • Check by converting back.

Question: Give two reasons why hexadecimal is used. [2]

Weak answer 0 marks

Computers understand hex better and it is faster.

Good answer 2 marks

It is easier for people to read and remember than binary, and there is less chance of making errors when copying it.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Computers only use binary – hex is for humans.
  • “Faster” needs explaining: quicker to type or write, because one digit replaces four bits.

Question: Normalise the floating point number 0.0011010 0100. [2]

Weak answer 1 mark

0.1101000 0110

Good answer 2 marks

Shift the mantissa left 2 places: 0.1101000. Subtract 2 from the exponent: 4 − 2 = 2, so 0010. Answer: 0.1101000 0010.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Shifting the mantissa left makes it bigger, so the exponent must go down.
  • Check: both forms should give the same denary value (3.25).

Question: State the operator and mask to clear the four least significant bits of a byte. [2]

Weak answer 1 mark

Use AND.

Good answer 2 marks

AND with the mask 1111 0000.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Give both the operator and the mask.
  • AND with 0 clears; AND with 1 keeps.

Question: Explain why an arithmetic shift is used to divide a negative two’s complement number by 2. [2]

Weak answer 0 marks

Because it is arithmetic.

Good answer 2 marks

The number is negative because its MSB is 1. An arithmetic shift copies the sign bit in on the left, so the result stays negative, whereas a logical shift would bring in a 0 and make it positive.

How to turn the weak answer into a good one:

  • Say what each shift puts into the MSB.
  • Link this to keeping the sign.

In the exam room

  • At the start, jot down the place values (128 … 1, and ½, ¼, ⅛, 1/16) and the hex digits A–F.
  • Write binary in groups of four (1101 0011) – fewer copying errors.
  • Always check conversions by converting back.
  • Practise little and often – see worked examples and spaced practice.
