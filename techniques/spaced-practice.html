Revision technique

Spaced practice and interleaving

Revise each topic several times with growing gaps, and mix topics within a session.

At a glance

  • Time: Plan once, follow for weeks
  • Best for: Every topic
  • You need: A calendar or revision timetable

Why it works

You forget quickly after learning something – this is the forgetting curve. Each time you revisit a topic, you top your memory back up, and it fades more slowly each time. Mixing topics in one session (interleaving) feels harder, but it trains you to choose the right method for each question, just as in the exam.

How to do it

  1. Start early. Cramming the night before gives the least lasting memory.
  2. Plan reviews for each topic: after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month.
  3. Use a different technique at each review: blurt, flashcards, questions.
  4. Mix topics in each session, including some from both papers.
  5. Use your red/amber/green ratings on the course content page to decide what comes up more often.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.1 All Paper 1 topics
  • 2.1.1 All Paper 2 topics

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Spacing and mixing will feel harder than cramming one topic. That effort is what makes the memory last – it doesn’t mean it isn’t working.
