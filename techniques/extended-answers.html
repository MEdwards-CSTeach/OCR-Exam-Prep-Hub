Revision technique

Planning extended answers

Plan long “discuss” and “evaluate” answers to a set structure so you hit every part of the mark scheme.

At a glance

  • Time: 10 minutes to plan, 15 to write
  • Best for: 9 and 12 mark questions
  • You need: Past questions and mark schemes

Why it works

Long “discuss” and “evaluate” questions carry the most marks. They are marked on levels: the top level needs accurate knowledge, applied to the scenario, with a reasoned conclusion. Planning to a structure means you cover all three every time instead of writing everything you know about the topic.

How to do it

  1. Underline the command word (discuss, evaluate, compare) and the context (who, what, why).
  2. Know it (AO1): list the key facts and terms you will use.
  3. Apply it (AO2): link each fact to the scenario. Use the names and details from the question.
  4. Judge it (AO3): weigh up both sides and reach a conclusion that answers the question.
  5. Self-mark with the mark scheme, then improve one paragraph.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.3 Choosing storage devices
  • 1.2.3 Choosing a development methodology
  • 1.3.3 Network security
  • 1.5.2 Moral and ethical issues
  • 2.1.5 Benefits and trade-offs of concurrency
  • 2.2.2 Suitability of computational methods

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Always use the scenario. A list of general advantages and disadvantages, without linking to the question, stays in the lower mark bands however much you write.

Command wordWhat it wants
DiscussExplore different sides of the issue, with reasons and examples, then conclude.
EvaluateWeigh up strengths and weaknesses, then make a judgement backed by evidence.
CompareGive similarities and differences – write about both things in every point.
JustifyGive reasons for a choice, linked to the scenario.

Examples

Example 1

Storage for a film company

1.1.3

Question: A small film company stores large video files. Staff edit videos in the office and send finished films to clients around the world. Discuss whether the company should use magnetic hard drives, solid state drives or cloud storage. [9]

Know it (AO1)Apply it (AO2)Judge it (AO3)
HDD: large capacity, low cost per GB, slower, moving partsVideos are large, so cheap capacity matters for storing old projectsGood for archives, but too slow for editing
SSD: fast access, no moving parts, higher cost per GBEditing large video needs fast read/write speedsBest for current projects being edited
Cloud: access anywhere, provider handles backup, needs internet, ongoing costClients are worldwide – easy to share finished films; off-site backupBest for sharing, but uploads of big files are slow and security depends on the provider

Model conclusion: “The company should use a combination. SSDs in the editing computers give the speed needed for editing large files. Finished films should go to cloud storage so clients anywhere can download them and there is an off-site backup. Old projects can be archived on cheaper HDDs. No single type meets all their needs.”

Example 2

Automated decision making for loans

1.5.2

Question: A bank plans to use an automated system to decide who gets a loan. Discuss the moral and ethical issues of this. [9]

Know it (AO1)Apply it (AO2)Judge it (AO3)
Fast, consistent decisions; works 24/7Customers get an instant answer; same rules applied to everyoneClear benefit to customers and to the bank’s costs
Bias in training data can be repeatedIf past loan data was biased against some groups, they could be unfairly refusedSerious ethical risk – must be tested for bias
Hard to explain how decisions are made; who is accountable?A refused customer may not understand why or be able to challenge itA human should review refusals
Uses personal data – Data Protection ActBank must use financial data only for the stated purpose and keep it secureLegal requirement, not optional
Jobs replaced by automationFewer loan advisers neededStaff could move to reviewing appeals

Model conclusion: “The system brings speed and consistency, but the risks of bias and unexplained decisions are serious because they affect people’s lives. It should be used only if it is tested for bias and customers can ask for a human to review a refusal.”
