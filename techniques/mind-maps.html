Revision technique

Mind maps

A diagram with the topic in the middle and branches for each sub-topic.

At a glance

  • Time: 20–30 minutes
  • Best for: Paper 1 topics with lots of facts
  • You need: Plain paper and coloured pens

Why it works

Your brain remembers pictures and connections better than long lists. Drawing a mind map makes you decide how the ideas fit together, so you understand the topic instead of just copying it. One page gives you the whole topic at a glance.

How to do it

  1. Put the topic in the middle of a landscape page, e.g. “1.1.3 Storage”.
  2. Add a thick branch for each spec point. Use the course content checklist so nothing is missed. One colour per branch.
  3. Add short key words on thinner branches – never full sentences.
  4. Add small drawings or symbols (a disc, a lightning bolt for fast) to help you remember.
  5. Test yourself: cover it, redraw it from memory, then check what you missed.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.3 Input, output and storage
  • 1.2.1 Systems software
  • 1.3.3 Networks
  • 1.5.2 Moral and ethical issues
  • 2.1.1 Thinking abstractly – to 2.1.5

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Don’t spend hours making it pretty. The learning happens when you decide what goes where and when you redraw it from memory.
