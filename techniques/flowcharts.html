Revision technique

Flowcharts and process diagrams

Draw a process as a series of steps, with arrows to show the order.

At a glance

  • Time: 15–25 minutes
  • Best for: Anything that happens in stages
  • You need: Paper, pencil, ruler

Why it works

Much of Computer Science is processes: things that happen in a set order. Drawing the steps makes the order stick, and a gap in your diagram shows you exactly which step you have forgotten. Exam questions often say “describe the steps…” – your flowchart is the answer plan.

How to do it

  1. Write the start and end of the process.
  2. List every step in order in its own box. Use a diamond for a decision (yes/no).
  3. Join the boxes with arrows. Loops go back to an earlier box.
  4. Add a short note beside each step explaining what happens and why.
  5. Redraw it from memory, then compare it with your notes.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.1 Fetch-decode-execute cycle, pipelining
  • 1.2.1 Interrupts, scheduling
  • 1.2.2 Stages of compilation
  • 1.2.3 Software development lifecycles
  • 1.3.3 DNS lookup, packet switching
  • 2.3.1 Sorting and searching algorithms

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Practise flowcharts for algorithms too, such as binary search or a stack push. Paper 2 can ask you to write or complete a flowchart.
