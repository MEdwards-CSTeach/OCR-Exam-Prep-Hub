Revision technique

Flashcards

A question or key term on the front, the answer on the back. Test yourself, again and again.

At a glance

  • Time: 10 minutes a day
  • Best for: Key terms, definitions, facts
  • You need: Index cards or a flashcard app

Why it works

Testing yourself – called retrieval practice – is one of the most effective ways to revise. Each time you pull an answer out of your memory, it becomes easier to recall next time. Reading the answer is not the same: say or write your answer before you flip.

How to do it

  1. One idea per card. Key term or question on the front, a short answer on the back.
  2. Answer out loud or on paper before you turn the card over.
  3. Sort the cards into “knew it” and “not yet” piles.
  4. Use the Leitner system (below) so you see hard cards more often.
  5. Mix decks from different topics once you know them well.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.1 Registers and buses
  • 1.3.2 Database terms and SQL keywords
  • 1.3.3 Protocols and network hardware
  • 1.4.1 Data types
  • 1.5.1 What each law covers
  • 2.3.1 Big O of each algorithm

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Make questions that need more than recall, e.g. front: “Why does more cache improve performance?” These practise AO2, not just AO1.
