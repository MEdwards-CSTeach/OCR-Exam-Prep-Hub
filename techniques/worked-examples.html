Revision technique

Worked examples and faded practice

Study a fully worked answer, then do more and more of the steps yourself.

At a glance

  • Time: 15–20 minutes
  • Best for: Calculations and methods with set steps
  • You need: Paper, pencil – no calculator

Why it works

When you are learning a method, a fully worked example is the fastest way to see every step. Then the help is faded out: you complete the last steps, then the whole question. This builds the method step by step without getting stuck – ideal for calculation questions where every step earns marks.

How to do it

  1. Study a worked example. Say why each step is done, not just what.
  2. Faded example: some steps are done for you. Complete the rest.
  3. Your turn: do a whole question with no help.
  4. Check your answer a different way, e.g. convert it back to denary.
  5. Repeat a few days later without looking at the worked example.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.4.1 Binary, hex, two’s complement, floating point, masks
  • 1.4.3 Karnaugh maps and Boolean simplification
  • 1.3.1 Run length encoding
  • 1.3.2 Normalisation to 3NF
  • 2.3.1 Dijkstra’s and A*

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: Exams are no-calculator. Practise with pencil and paper, and always show your working – method marks are available even if the final answer is wrong.

Examples

Example 1

Two’s complement: negative numbers in 8 bits

1.4.1

Step 1 – Worked example: −45

① Write +45 in binary: 0010 1101

② Flip every bit: 1101 0010

③ Add 1: 1101 0011

✔ Check: −128 + 64 + 16 + 2 + 1 = −45

Step 2 – Faded: −20

① +20 in binary: 0001 0100

② Flip every bit: 1110 1011

Step 3 – Your turn: −99

Example 2

Normalising floating point numbers

1.4.1

8-bit mantissa and 4-bit exponent, both in two’s complement. A normalised positive mantissa starts 0.1. A normalised negative mantissa starts 1.0.

Step 1 – Worked example: mantissa 0.0011010, exponent 0101

① Positive, so it must start 0.1. Move the bits left 2 places: 0.1101000

② Moving left makes the number bigger, so subtract 2 from the exponent: 5 − 2 = 3 → 0011

✔ Check: 0.0011010 × 25 = 6.5 and 0.1101 × 23 = 6.5

Step 2 – Faded: mantissa 0.0001100, exponent 0110

① Move left 3 places: 0.1100000

Step 3 – Your turn (negative!): mantissa 1.1110100, exponent 0011

Hint for the negative one

A negative mantissa must start 1.0. Remove the extra 1s after the point by shifting left, and fill with 0s on the right. Count the places moved.
