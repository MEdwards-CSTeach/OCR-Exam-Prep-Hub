Revision technique

Comparison tables

Put two or three similar things side by side and compare them point by point.

At a glance

  • Time: 15–20 minutes
  • Best for: Topics with pairs or groups of similar things
  • You need: Ruled paper or a spreadsheet

Why it works

Exams love “compare”, “differences between” and “which is more suitable” questions. A table makes you match each point on one side with its opposite on the other, so your answers are balanced and you don’t only write about one side.

How to do it

  1. Choose the things to compare, e.g. CISC and RISC. Put them as column headings.
  2. Choose the comparison points as rows: speed, cost, complexity, typical use.
  3. Fill in every cell. Each row should show a clear difference.
  4. Add a “typical use” row – it helps with “justify your choice” questions.
  5. Cover one column and try to recall it from the other.

Best topics for this technique

  • 1.1.2 CISC vs RISC
  • 1.1.3 Magnetic vs flash vs optical, RAM vs ROM
  • 1.2.2 Translators, open vs closed source
  • 1.2.3 Development methodologies
  • 1.3.1 Lossy vs lossless, symmetric vs asymmetric
  • 2.3.1 Sorting algorithms and their Big O

These topics suit this technique well, but you can use it for any topic.

Tip: In the exam, turn each row into a sentence that uses a comparative word: “RISC instructions are simpler than CISC instructions, so they usually take one clock cycle.”
