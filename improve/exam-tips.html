Exam tips and improving your grade

OCR A Level Computer Science • H446

Exam tips

How to read questions, use your time well and avoid losing easy marks. These tips help at every grade.

Top three tips

  1. Read the question twice.
  2. Let the marks guide how much you write.
  3. Use the context in the question.

Manage your time

Both papers are 2 hours 30 minutes for 140 marks. That is just over 1 minute per mark.

Question worthSpend about
2 marks2 minutes
4 marks4–5 minutes
9 marks10 minutes
12 marks13–14 minutes

Stuck? Leave a gap, mark it with a star in the margin and move on. Come back at the end. Never leave a question blank – a sensible attempt can still earn marks.

Read the question: command words

The command word tells you what kind of answer gets the marks. Underline it before you start writing.

Command wordWhat to doExample
State / Identify / GiveA short answer – a word, phrase or fact. No explanation needed.State one purpose of the BIOS.
DescribeSay what something is or does, with detail. Usually one point plus a bit more for each mark.Describe how a hash table stores data.
ExplainGive reasons: say why or how. Use “because”, “so”, “which means”.Explain why a GPU is suited to machine learning.
CompareSimilarities and differences. Mention both things in every point.Compare a stack and a queue.
DiscussLook at different sides with reasons, linked to the context, then conclude.Discuss the use of cloud storage by a school.
EvaluateWeigh up strengths and weaknesses, then make a judgement backed by reasons.Evaluate the use of a merge sort for this data.
JustifyGive reasons for a choice, linked to the scenario.Justify the choice of a binary search.
Write / CompleteProduce code or pseudocode, or finish an algorithm. Logic matters more than exact syntax.Write a function that returns the largest item.
Calculate / ConvertWork out an answer. Show every step – there are marks for method.Convert −45 to 8-bit two’s complement.
TraceFollow an algorithm and record each change of value, usually in a table.Complete the trace table for the algorithm.

The marks tell you how much to write. A 2-mark “describe” usually needs two linked points. A 4-mark “explain” usually needs two points, each with a reason.

How to approach each type of question

Short answer (1–3 marks)

  • One clear point per mark.
  • Use the exact key term.
  • Don’t write a paragraph for a 1-mark question.

Calculations

  • Write every step – method marks are available.
  • Check by converting back.
  • No calculators, so practise by hand.

Trace tables

  • Work one line at a time.
  • Only write a value when it changes.
  • Watch loop limits and < vs <=.

Writing algorithms

  • Plan the steps in English first.
  • Use clear names and indentation.
  • Check edge cases: empty list, item not found.

Extended response (9–12 marks)

  • Spend a minute planning.
  • Know it → apply it → judge it.
  • End with a conclusion that answers the question.

Paper 2 Section B scenario

  • Read the scenario carefully before starting.
  • Underline key facts and names.
  • Use them in every answer.

Common ways to lose marks

  • Vague words like “faster”, “better”, “easier” with no reason. Faster than what? Why?
  • Brand names instead of the general term – write “a cloud storage service”, not a product name.
  • Repeating the question back as the answer.
  • Ignoring the scenario and writing a general answer.
  • Contradicting yourself – if you give two answers and one is wrong, you may lose the mark.
  • No working on calculations.
  • Spending too long on one question.

Before and on the day

  • Do timed past papers and mark them with the mark scheme.
  • Read the pseudocode guide (spec appendix 5d). Any clear style is accepted, but you need to read OCR’s style.
  • Bring several black pens, a pencil and a ruler. No calculator.
  • Use the 5 minutes at the start to look through the whole paper.
  • Write neatly. If an answer continues elsewhere, say where.
  • Use spare time to check calculations and trace tables.

Check yourself

Tick what you already do in practice papers.
